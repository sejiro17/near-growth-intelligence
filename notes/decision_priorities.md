# Decision Priorities

## Purpose

This research helps readers decide which growth claims are supported, which need further investigation, and what evidence would change the assessment.

The priorities below reflect my interpretation of the eight-week findings. They are not automated scores, application rankings or buy-and-sell signals.

## 1. Check Account Lifecycle Before Interpreting Participation

During the spike week, 309,280 accounts absent the following week had successfully executed receipts containing self-deletion actions. They represented 82.53% of the absent group.

Decision implication: the following-week absence rate should not be used as an ordinary user-churn measure or as sufficient evidence that an application failed to retain users.

Recommended action: examine deletion and recreation alongside account roles before evaluating acquisition or retention.

Evidence: results 11, 16 and 17.

## 2. Investigate Execution Quality Behind Growth

Transactions outside the first week's ten leading signing accounts increased 52.50%. However, one investigated growth-contributing account, tinykoala595.near, had 715,518 failed initial receipts, representing 91.90% of its transactions.

Those failed receipts accounted for approximately 211.1629 NEAR in recorded initial-receipt execution costs.

Decision implication: additional submissions and execution spending do not necessarily represent successful application interactions.

Recommended action: use receipt outcomes and diagnostic errors alongside transaction growth when selecting activity for further investigation. The sampled slippage and deposit errors provide specific leads, but do not establish network-wide error frequencies.

Evidence: results 21–27.

## 3. Separate Infrastructure Concentration From Independent Demand

The ten busiest signing accounts generated 48.17%–67.76% of weekly transactions. Leading-account action records included substantial delegated activity as well as direct contract calls.

Decision implication: signing-account concentration alone cannot establish how many independent participants or applications are being served.

Recommended action: verify account roles and delegated interactions before treating concentrated signing activity as either broad adoption or weak demand.

Evidence: results 10 and 18–20.

## 4. Follow Growth Beyond the Original Leaders

The first week's ten busiest signing accounts recorded a 19.83% decline in transactions between the first and final weeks. All other accounts combined recorded a 52.50% increase.

Accounts outside every weekly top-twenty signing-account ranking contributed 43.80% of that outside group's net increase.

Decision implication: the original leaders do not explain all observed growth. Activity beyond them deserves attention, while its ownership, persistence and execution quality remain incompletely established.

Recommended action: examine those contributors before allocating ecosystem support on headline growth alone. Growth contribution identifies investigation candidates, not proven application quality.

Evidence: results 18, 21–24.

## 5. Require Economic Reconciliation Before Making Supply Claims

Between the first and final weeks, receipt execution outcomes increased 66.85%, recorded receipt execution costs increased 82.73%, and average recorded cost per receipt increased 9.52%.

Observed total supply increased approximately 4.936 million NEAR across the full period. Validator-update balance changes did not fully reconcile with supply movements.

Decision implication: higher execution spending does not establish permanent token burning, protocol revenue or value accruing to holders.

Recommended action: keep execution costs and supply movement visible as separate measures. Any claim about issuance, burning or token-holder value should identify the relevant flows and address the unresolved reconciliation difference.

Evidence: results 13, 14 and 28–34.

## Application to Decisions

| Reader | Decision supported | Current evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Investors and research analysts | Decide which adoption and economic claims warrant further due diligence | No valuation, expected return or complete token-value model |
| NEAR ecosystem teams | Identify activity requiring lifecycle, concentration or execution-quality investigation before allocating support | No verified ranking of application impact |
| Application developers | Identify diagnostic questions and compare activity with execution outcomes | Selected accounts and sampled errors do not represent every application or user |
